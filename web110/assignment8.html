For Activity 1, Internet Safety and Security Tutorials, I focused on learning about internet safety and common online threats. I learned about viruses, worms, Trojan horses, ransomware, spyware, phishing, and denial-of-service attacks. These threats can damage computers, steal personal information, or prevent users from accessing their files. I also learned the importance of practicing safe browsing habits, avoiding suspicious links, and downloading files only from trusted websites. This activity helped me understand that being careful online is an important part of protecting my personal information and computer. For Activity 2, Web Browser Security and Privacy Settings, I reviewed browser security and privacy settings. I learned how to check for browser updates, update or disable unnecessary extensions, clear the browser cache, and remove browsing history and cookies. I also reviewed autofill settings to understand how browsers may save personal information. Visiting a secure website and examining its security certificate helped me understand how HTTPS encrypts information exchanged between a browser and a website. Additionally, I explored private or incognito browsing and learned that it prevents some browsing information from being saved locally after the session ends. However, it does not make users completely anonymous online. These practices can help improve privacy and reduce security risks while browsing the internet. For Activity 3, Malware Protection, Password Managers, and Firewalls, I focused on protecting computers and online accounts from security threats. I learned that antivirus and anti-malware software can detect, quarantine, and remove malicious programs. I also researched the EICAR test file, which is designed to test antivirus detection without using real malware. In addition, I learned that firewalls help protect computers and networks by filtering network traffic and blocking certain unauthorized connections. Another important topic was password managers and multifactor authentication (MFA). Password managers help users create and store strong, unique passwords, while MFA adds another layer of protection when signing in to accounts. Enabling MFA on email, social media, and financial accounts can help reduce the risk of unauthorized access.
