For Activity 1, I downloaded and compared several browsers (Firefox, Brave, and Google Chrome), starting with Brave. Brave has built-in ad blocking, tracker blocking, cookie blocking, and privacy protection. Since Brave is a Chromium-based browser, it supports Chrome extensions. The issues that come with Brave are mostly due to its successes in things like ad blocking, causing some websites to behave oddly, and if you're heavily invested in Google's ecosystem, Chrome is more seamless. Firefox is your best choice if you want a browser that isn't Chromium-based. Mozilla uses its very own Gecko engine that emphasizes things like privacy and user control. The main issue with Firefox is that websites are commonly more optimized for Chromium and having a smaller extension ecosystem. Finally, we move to Chrome. Chrome is a compatibility and ecosystem powerhouse since its chromium based and extremely widely supported, and integrates very well with Google services. The issue with Chrome is that it's much less privacy-focused, more integrated with Google's data ecosystem, and has a very unaggressive ad blocker. For Activity 2, I configured the browser settings of the browsers used in Activity 1 (Brave, Firefox, Google Chrome). Brave’s settings align more with privacy, with the ability to change privacy settings, including things like blocking ads, trackers, and changing individual website settings. Firefox provides strong privacy protection in its settings, similar to Brave but allowing more user customization of the browser, and also managing user passwords, cookies, and extensions. Chrome settings allow customization and syncing across devices and help to manage privacy, passwords, and overall security. Overall, each browser has relatively similar settings, allowing you to control privacy, security, and your overall browsing experience Activity 3 was configuring browser extensions again with Brave, Firefox, and Google Chrome. Brave and Chrome both support the same extensions, as they both use Chromium. Brave and Chrome extension settings let you enable, disable, remove, and manage permissions. Firefox extension settings allow you to control updates, remove extensions, and manage what each extension can access. Overall, Firefox has a harder time finding extensions, as it doesn't use Chromium like Brave and Chrome, meaning it can't use most things available on the Chrome Web Store.
