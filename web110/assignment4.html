For Activity 1, I reviewed how to send, receive, and respond to emails. I learned how to use an email account to send, receive, and respond to messages. As part of this activity, I practiced composing an email by entering a recipient, adding a subject, writing a message, sending it, and responding to an email from another person. I also reviewed the basic features and purpose of email communication. This activity helped me understand how email can be used for both personal and professional communication. I can apply what I learned by using email to communicate with my employer and coworkers. I also learned the importance of writing clear subject lines, using an appropriate tone, and checking the recipient before sending a message. These skills will be useful for school assignments, future job applications, workplace communication, and everyday life. For Activity 2, I used an email client program. I reviewed information about Mozilla Thunderbird and learned how an email client can be used instead of accessing email exclusively through a web browser. I learned about setting up an email account in Thunderbird, configuring the account to connect with an email provider, and using the program to send and receive messages. This activity gave me a better understanding of how email applications communicate with an email service through account settings and mail protocols. Personally, I can apply this knowledge by using an email client to organize messages from an account in one desktop application. I can also use Thunderbird’s features to manage folders, read messages, compose emails, and keep track of correspondence. Understanding email clients is useful because it gives me another way to access and manage email and helps me become more comfortable troubleshooting basic account and connection settings. For Activity 3, I viewed email message headers. I learned how to view technical headers associated with an email message and reviewed information about email spoofing. Email headers contain information that can help identify where a message came from and how it was processed by mail servers. I learned to examine information such as the From address, Return-Path, Received lines, and authentication results. I also learned that the visible From address alone doesn't necessarily prove that a message was genuinely sent by that address because attackers can spoof email addresses. This activity is personally useful because it improves my ability to recognize suspicious or potentially fraudulent or fake messages. I can apply these skills when receiving unexpected emails, especially messages requesting money, passwords, account information, or urgent action. Checking email headers and authentication information can provide additional evidence about a message’s authenticity and help me make safer decisions when using email.
